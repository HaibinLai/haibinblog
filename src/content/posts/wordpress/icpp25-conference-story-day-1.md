<h3>Carbon-Aware Workflow Scheduling with Fixed Mapping and Deadline Constraint</h3>
<p>Large data and computing centers consume a significant share of the world’s energy consumption. A prominent subset of the workloads in such centers are workflows with interdependent tasks, usually represented as directed acyclic graphs (DAGs). **To reduce the carbon emissions resulting from executing such workflows in centers with a mixed (renewable and non-renewable) energy supply, it is advisable to move task executions to time intervals with sufficient green energy when possible.</p>
<p><strong>To this end, we formalize the above problem as a scheduling problem with a given mapping and ordering of the tasks. We show that this problem can be solved in polynomial time in the uniprocessor case.</strong> For at least two processors, however, the problem becomes NP-hard. Hence, we propose a heuristic framework called CaWoSched that combines several greedy approaches with local search.** To assess the 16 heuristics resulting from different combinations, we also devise a simple baseline algorithm and an exact ILP-based solution. Our experimental results show that our heuristics provide significant savings in carbon emissions compared to the baseline.</p>

<h3>HHOTuner: Efficient Performance Tuning with Harris Hawks Optimization</h3>
<p>iowa state University</p>
<p>NSF</p>
<p><strong>An auto-tuning framework for large space optuning</strong></p>
<p>Autotuning: Code+Arch+Tune = Best performance</p>
<p>They try to use an evolutionary algorithm call <strong>Harris Hawks</strong></p>
<p>This is a very very cooooool figure</p>
<p><img src="https://blog-1327458544.cos.ap-guangzhou.myqcloud.com/N2025/20250909183546528.png" alt="05cc251ea84fab7bc67c86aab8d816de.jpg" /></p>
<p>Harris Howk Algorithm idea</p>
<p>Main Ideas</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Exploration phase</strong>:
<ul>
<li>Hawks search the environment (global exploration of the solution space).</li>
<li>Two strategies: random perching or scanning for potential prey.</li>
</ul></li>
<li><strong>Exploitation phase</strong>:
<ul>
<li>Once prey is spotted, hawks begin <strong>encircling and besieging</strong> it (local exploitation).</li>
<li>Several strategies are used: “soft siege,” “hard siege,” or random surprise pounces.</li>
</ul></li>
<li><strong>Energy parameter (E)</strong>:
<ul>
<li>Represents the prey’s escaping energy, which decreases over time.</li>
<li>When ∣E∣≥1|E| ≥ 1∣E∣≥1, exploration dominates.</li>
<li>When ∣E∣&lt;1|E| &lt; 1∣E∣&lt;1, exploitation dominates.</li>
</ul></li>
<li><strong>Surprise pounce mechanism</strong>:
<ul>
<li>Mimics the unpredictable behavior of prey escaping.</li>
<li>Introduces randomness to help the algorithm escape local optima.</li>
</ul></li>
</ol>

<h3>One GPU, Many Ranks: Enabling Performance and Energy-Efficient In-Transit Visualization via Resource Sharing</h3>
<p>ANL</p>
<p>TL;DR from Haibin: they use MIG/MPS/Time Sharing on GPU to accerlate a HPC workflow.</p>
<p>In-transit visualization has become essential in high-performance computing (HPC) to reduce I/O overheads and enable real-time data analysis. However, as simulations grow in scale and complexity, these visualization tasks increasingly demand substantial computational resources, exacerbating energy consumption and limiting system scalability. <strong>As we show in this paper, a key bottleneck is the conventional one-rank-per-GPU allocation model, which leads to irregular GPU utilization and waste of hardware resources.</strong> </p>
<p><strong>To tackle this challenge, we propose using GPU-sharing strategies to improve energy efficiency in in-transit visualization without compromising performance. We evaluate six distinct configurations built upon three NVIDIA GPU-sharing mechanisms: the default CUDA model with context switching between processes, Multi-Process Service (MPS), which enables dynamic context sharing, and Multi-Instance GPU (MIG), which provides hardware-level partitioning.</strong> Using the WarpX simulation code and Ascent visualization framework, our experiments on the Polaris supercomputer span multiple rendering techniques, node counts, and data sizes. </p>
<p><strong>Results show that GPU-sharing strategies can improve the trade-off between performance and energy, represented by the energy-delay product (EDP) metric, by up to 81.7%.</strong> We also show that workload-aware strategy selection is essential to improve performance-energy efficiency: MIG-based configurations are more effective for lightweight and regular workloads, offering up to 64.5% energy savings, while MPS better handles GPU-intensive workloads, achieving up to 71.1% EDP improvement. Finally, we demonstrate that optimized sharing strategies can reduce the required compute nodes by up to 75%, freeing system resources for concurrent workloads.</p>
